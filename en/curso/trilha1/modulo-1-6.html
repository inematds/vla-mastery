🔢 Action Tokenization: Discrete vs Continuous vs FAST

How to represent actions for the model is just as important as the architecture. From continuous regression to RT-2’s discrete binning, to the long-sequence problem at high frequency, and finally FAST—DCT+BPE tokenization that compresses in the frequency domain. The technical close of Track 1.

Detailed content

Three paths to the same signal: continuous floats, redundant tokens per timestep, or a few compressed tokens via DCT (FAST).

1

📈 Continuous action: direct regression

The most natural representation: the head outputs floats directly for each action dimension (Δx, Δy, Δz, rotation, gripper). It’s simple, preserves full resolution, and wastes nothing on discretization. The problem, as seen in Module 1.4, is that when trained with MSE/L1 it is unimodal — collapses modes. That’s why continuous regression shines only when paired with an expressive head (diffusion/flow), rather than a simple MLP+MSE.

📊 When pure continuous works

  • Unimodal tasks — a single clear solution per state.
  • Minimum latency — one forward pass, no denoising steps.
  • Baseline — starting point for measuring gains from the other options.

Regression

2

🔲 Discrete binning (RT-1/RT-2)

RT-1, RT-2, and OpenVLA discretize each dimension into uniform bins (RT-1 uses 256) and treat prediction as classification via cross-entropy. The advantage: it becomes a token problem, integrating seamlessly with the LLM’s vocabulary and capturing multimodality (categorical distribution). The drawbacks: loss of resolution (quantization) and loss of correlation between dimensions, since each one is tokenized independently.

✓ Advantages of binning

  • ✓Integrates into the LLM vocabulary.
  • ✓Captures multimodality via softmax.
  • ✓Simple autoregressive training (cross-entropy).

✗ Disadvantages

  • ✗Resolution limited by the number of bins.
  • ✗Ignores correlation between dimensions.
  • ✗Long sequences at high frequency (next topic).

3

🌊 The high-frequency problem

This is where the pain that motivated FAST lives. At 50Hz, actions at consecutive timesteps are almost identical — the arm moves very little in 20ms. Tokenizing each timestep generates redundant tokens and enormous sequences: a 1-second chunk at 50Hz with 7 dimensions contains 350 tokens, most of them repeated. Long sequences make autoregressive training prohibitively expensive and degrade learning. Naive binning simply doesn't scale to high-frequency dynamic control.

# Custo de binning por timestep a 50Hz
chunk = 1s · 50Hz = 50 timesteps
dims  = 7 (xyz + rot + gripper)
tokens = 50 × 7 = 350  ← maioria redundante
# treino autoregressivo nessa sequência: lento e instável

⚡ Practical tip

Redundancy is a clue: if the signal varies slowly between timesteps, it's compressible. Smooth signals concentrate energy in a few low frequencies—exactly what the DCT exploits in FAST.

4

⚡ FAST: DCT + BPE tokenization

FAST (Frequency-space Action Sequence Tokenization, Physical Intelligence, 2025) reverses the logic: instead of tokenizing in time, it transforms the trajectory into frequency domain via DCT (the same transform as JPEG), quantize the coefficients, and compress them with BPE. Since smooth signals concentrate energy in a few low frequencies, the result is few high-fidelity tokens. FAST makes autoregressive VLAs competitive with diffusion in high-frequency control—RT-2/OpenVLA-style, but dexterous.

1

Transforms the action chunk into frequency coefficients.

2

Quantize and discard

Keeps the dominant coefficients (low frequency), zeros out the rest.

3

Compress with BPE

Byte-Pair Encoding groups patterns into a few dense tokens.

💡 Why FAST matters

FAST drastically reduces sequence length while maintaining fidelity, speeding up training and enabling autoregressive VLAs for dexterous tasks. It is the bridge between the simplicity of the token paradigm and the high-frequency demands of fine manipulation.

⚖️ Trade-offs: length × fidelity × speed

Every tokenization lives in a Pareto frontier: fewer tokens train faster but may lose fidelity; more tokens preserve detail but cost more in training and latency. Naive binning lands in a bad spot (many tokens, average fidelity). FAST shifts the frontier — few tokens AND high fidelity — by exploiting the signal's frequency structure. The right choice depends on how compressible your signal is.

Esquema     Tokens/chunk   Fidelidade   Treino
contínuo    1 (vetor)      total        rápido*  (*precisa head expressiva)
binning     muitos         média        lento
FAST        poucos         alta         rápido

📊 The FAST gain

  • ↓ length — orders of magnitude fewer tokens than timestep binning.
  • ↑ speed — much faster autoregressive training.
  • = fidelity — quality comparable to diffusion on dexterous tasks.

🎛 Choose by use case and impact on the action head

There is no universal tokenization—the choice shapes the entire action head. Slow, quasi-static task (paused pick-and-place)? Binning works. Dexterous, dynamic manipulation (folding clothes, insertions)? FAST (autoregressive) or diffusion/flow. The representation choice determines whether the head is a detokenizer, a generative action expert, or a regressor—closing the conceptual loop that began in Module 1.2.

1

Characterize the task

Frequency, dynamics, and multimodality required.

2

Choose the representation

Binning, FAST, or continuous+diffusion, depending on the profile.

3

Match the head

Detokenizer, action expert, or regressor—consistent with the representation.

⚡ Practical tip

In 2026, two paths win in practice: FAST + autoregressive backbone (simple, fast training) or flow matching (fluid, continuous action). Start with what your stack already supports and iterate.
