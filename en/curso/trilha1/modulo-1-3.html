📈 Model Evolution: RT-1, RT-2, OpenVLA, π0, Qwen-VLA

The VLA family tree over four years. From RT-1 with actions in bins, to RT-2 inheriting the web, to the openness of Octo and OpenVLA, to π0's continuous flow matching and the giant foundation models of 2025. Each generation solved a bottleneck from the previous one.

Detailed content

Each step solves the previous bottleneck: web knowledge, openness, continuous action, and increasingly large datasets.

1

🟢 RT-1 (2022): transformer + FiLM

RT-1 (Robotics Transformer 1) proved the basic formula: images pass through an EfficientNet conditioned by FiLM (instruction feature modulation), TokenLearner compresses tokens, and a transformer predicts actions discretized into 256 bins per dimension. Trained on ~130k demos across 700+ tasks with 13 robots over 17 months, it achieved ~97% success on seen tasks and modest generalization. It was proof of concept that tokenizing actions works at scale.

📊 RT-1 in numbers

  • ~130k teleoperation demonstrations.
  • 700+ distinct instructions, 13 robots.
  • 256 bins per action dimension · 35M parameters.

2

🔵 RT-2 (2023): co-fine-tuned VLM

RT-2 started from web-scale VLMs (PaLI-X up to 55B, PaLM-E) and co-fine-tuned them with robot data, treating actions as text strings in the same vocabulary. The co-fine-tuning (mixing web and robot data) preserves internet knowledge, generating emergent semantic generalization: choosing "the object that doesn't belong" or "the flag of country X." RT-2 also demonstrated chain-of-thought robotic, reasoning in text before acting.

✓ RT-2 gains

  • ✓Semantic generalization to novel objects/instructions.
  • ✓Multi-step reasoning via chain-of-thought.
  • ✓Reuses the scale of web pretraining.

✗ Limitations

  • ✗Closed: weights and code not released.
  • ✗Expensive inference (up to 55B), difficult in real time.
  • ✗Discrete actions limit fine, dynamic control.

🟡 Octo & OpenVLA (2024): open-source

2024 democratized the paradigm. Octo (Berkeley) is a small transformer (27M-93M) with a diffusion head, trained on Open X-Embodiment and easy to fine-tune. OpenVLA-7B (Stanford/Berkeley) uses a Prismatic VLM (Llama 2-7B + SigLIP/DINOv2) trained on ~970k OXE trajectories, outperforming RT-2-X with 7× fewer parameters. The key point: both allow efficient fine-tuning via LoRA and quantization, running on a GPU.

# Fine-tunar OpenVLA com LoRA (esquema)

⚡ Practical tip

For most real-world projects, start with OpenVLA + LoRA on your own demos. It’s the shortest path from "I have an arm" to "I have a policy," without training a 7B model from scratch.

🌊 π0 / π0.5 (Physical Intelligence)

π0 broke away from discrete tokenization. On top of a base PaliGemma, couples a action expert trained by flow matching that generates continuous actions in chunks at up to 50Hz — ideal for dexterous tasks like folding clothes. π0.5 advances open-world generalization by training on heterogeneous data (including web data) for long-horizon tasks in homes it has never seen. It is the open state of the art in generalist manipulation for 2025.

🧠 Qwen-VLA and the 2025 family

2025 brought VLAs built on Increasingly larger and more capable VLMs — families based on Qwen2-VL and similar models. The bet: the better the base model's multimodal reasoning, the better the VLA performs on tasks that require understanding scenes, counting, and spatial relationships. The concept of embodied reasoning — the model verbalizes a plan and grounds it in action. Models like Gemini Robotics-ER and Qwen-VLA variants exemplify this convergence of strong reasoning and control.

📊 Trends in 2025-2026

  • Larger bases — Reasoning VLMs (Qwen2-VL, Gemini) as the core.
  • Embodied reasoning — plan in language before acting.
  • Dual-system — separate slow reasoning from fast control (see Track 3).

💡 Why scale the base model

The intuition is that the bottleneck for complex tasks is no longer the action head, but the understanding the scene and the objective. Larger bases carry better priors—as long as there's enough action data to ground them.

📊 Comparative timeline

Summarizing the lineage across five dimensions — size, data, tokenization, license, and focus. The trend is clear: larger bases, increasingly continuous/expressive action, and a divide between closed frontier models (RT-2, Gemini) and practical open models (Octo, OpenVLA, π0).

⚡ Practical tip

When choosing, weigh license + inference cost + action type. Slow task and limited demos? OpenVLA. Dexterous, fluid manipulation? π0 with flow matching.
