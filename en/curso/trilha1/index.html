Module 1.1 ~35 min · Beginner

🌐 The Big Picture: From LLMs to Robots That Act

Why LLMs alone can’t interact with the world, what embodied AI is, and why 2026 is the moment for foundation models in robotics.

What it is: the chain that transforms pixels and instructions into motor torque. LLMs dominate the language stage but don't close the loop with the physical world.

Why learn: understanding where the “action” bottleneck lies is what separates a chatbot from a robot.

Key concepts: grounding, embodiment, sensorimotor loop.

What it is: abstract reasoning is easy for machines; sensorimotor skills are hard. Manipulation unlocks AI’s oldest bottleneck.

Why learn: explains why "picking up a cup" is harder than playing chess.

Key concepts: Moravec’s paradox, contact, dexterity gap.

What it is: the generalization thesis—a model trained on many tasks and bodies outperforms N specialized policies.

Why learn: is the bet driving Google, PI, and NVIDIA.

Key concepts: generalist policy, cross-embodiment, positive transfer.

What it is: co-fine-tuning a web-scale VLM transfers semantic knowledge to action (generalization to objects never seen in demos).

Why learn: marks the birth of the modern VLA paradigm.

Key concepts: web→action transfer, semantic generalization, emergent skills.

What it is: Google DeepMind, Physical Intelligence, NVIDIA, Figure, and Tesla — who does what and why.

Why learn: mapping the players helps guide your career and paper reading.

Key concepts: GR00T, π0, Helix, Optimus.

What it is: the convergence of Open X-Embodiment, GPUs, and transformers/diffusion made VLAs viable.

Why learn: understanding the “why 2026” helps anticipate the next wave.

Key concepts: Open X-Embodiment, scaling, Pareto moment.

Module 1.2 ~40 min · Beginner

🧬 VLA Anatomy: Vision + Language + Action

The inner components of a VLA: vision encoder, language backbone, multimodal fusion, action head, proprioception, and control loop.

What it is: image patches become visual tokens; DINOv2 provides spatial features, SigLIP aligns vision and text.

Why learn: the encoder choice defines what the robot “sees”.

Key concepts: patch embedding, dual encoder, frozen backbone.

What it is: a pretrained LLM becomes the reasoning core that integrates instructions, context, and vision.

Why learn: is where semantic generalization lives.

Key concepts: PaliGemma, Prismatic, transformer decoder.

What it is: how image and text tokens enter the same context — concatenated (early) or via cross-attention.

Why learn: defines efficiency and grounding capacity.

Key concepts: early fusion, cross-attention, token budget.

What it is: converts the backbone's latent state into motor commands — end-effector Δpose and gripper.

Why learn: is where diffusion, tokens, and regression compete.

Key concepts: action expert, detokenizer, end-effector delta.

What it is: joints, force, and temporal history are included as additional observations beyond the camera.

Why learn: without state, the robot "forgets" where it is.

Key concepts: proprioception, observation history, state token.

What it is: inference frequency, latency, and action-chunk prediction with a receding horizon.

Why learn: poorly handled latency kills the real-world task.

Key concepts: action chunking, prediction horizon, control rate.

Module 1.3 ~45 min · Intermediate

📈 Model Evolution: RT-1, RT-2, OpenVLA, π0, Qwen-VLA

The VLA lineage from 2022 to 2026, including sizes, data, tokenization, licenses, and the architectural leaps of each generation.

What it is: transformer with FiLM conditioning, discretized actions in bins, trained on ~130k demos.

Why learn: is the foundation that proved actions could be tokenized.

Key concepts: FiLM, action binning, EfficientNet tokens.

What it is: actions become text tokens in a VLM (PaLI-X/PaLM-E), with web+robot co-fine-tuning.

Why learn: ushered in emergent semantic generalization.

Key concepts: action-as-text, co-fine-tuning, robotic chain-of-thought.

What it is: OpenVLA-7B (Prismatic VLM + OXE) and Octo open up the paradigm with efficient fine-tuning via LoRA.

Why learn: are the models you can actually run.

Key concepts: Prismatic, OXE, LoRA, quantization.

What it is: Generalist VLA with an action expert via flow matching, continuous action at 50Hz, PaliGemma as the base.

Why learn: is the state of the art in fluid manipulation.

Key concepts: flow matching, action expert, open-world.

What it is: Large VLMs (Qwen2-VL and similar models) as the base for VLAs that combine reasoning and action.

Why learn: shows the trend toward increasingly larger datasets.

Key concepts: reasoning-VLA, base scaling, embodied reasoning.

What it is: size × data × tokenization × license × performance table for the main VLAs.

Why learn: choosing a model requires comparing trade-offs.

Key concepts: benchmark, license, parameters vs. data.

Module 1.4 ~40 min · Intermediate

🎯 Imitation Learning and Behavior Cloning

How robots learn from human demonstrations, why behavior cloning breaks down over time, and what to do about it.

What it is: supervised state→action regression that copies the demonstrator.

Why learn: 90% of VLAs train with BC.

Key concepts: supervised policy, π(a|s), maximum likelihood.

What it is: small errors take the robot out of the training distribution, and the error accumulates quadratically over the horizon.

Why learn: is the number one failure of naive BC.

Key concepts: covariate shift, compounding error, O(T²).

What it is: DAgger collects corrections in states visited by the policy; teleoperation generates the human demos.

Why learn: collection quality sets the policy’s ceiling.

Key concepts: DAgger, on-policy correction, leader-follower.

What it is: multiple valid actions for the same state; the average (MSE) falls in the gap between them.

Why learn: justifies diffusion and expressive policies.

Key concepts: multimodal actions, mode averaging, expressive policy.

What it is: diversity of objects, positions, and optimal vs. suboptimal demonstrations matter more than raw volume.

Why learn: bad data limits any architecture.

Key concepts: coverage, diversity, demo quality.

What it is: Success rate is the north star, but generalization to new objects/positions/instructions is the real test.

Why learn: the wrong metric misleads.

Key concepts: success rate, OOD eval, generalization axes.

Module 1.5 ~50 min · Advanced

🌫️ Diffusion Policy and Flow Matching

The math that generates multimodal actions from noise: conditioned DDPM, score matching, flow matching, and inference cost.

What it is: diffusion captures multimodal distributions by denoising entire trajectories.

Why learn: solves the MSE mode averaging.

Key concepts: denoising, multimodal, trajectory generation.

What it is: Observation-conditioned DDPM, with action chunking and receding horizon.

Why learn: became the fine manipulation baseline.

Key concepts: DDPM, receding horizon, U-Net/transformer policy.

What it is: learn the density gradient (score) to reverse the noise; conditioning via FiLM/cross-attention.

Why learn: is the mathematical engine of diffusion.

Key concepts: score function, reverse process, conditioning.

What it is: learn a vector field that transports noise→action via an ODE; simpler and more stable training.

Why learn: is what makes π0 flow at 50Hz.

Key concepts: flow matching, ODE, conditional vector field.

What it is: the number of denoising steps becomes latency; consistency models and distillation reduce it to a few steps.

Why learn: real time requires few NFEs.

Key concepts: NFE, consistency model, distillation.

What it is: trade-off between diffusion's expressiveness and the simplicity/latency of autoregressive tokens.

Why learn: dictates the choice of action head.

Key concepts: expressiveness × latency, AR vs. diffusion.

Module 1.6 ~45 min · Advanced

🔢 Action Tokenization: Discrete vs Continuous vs FAST

How to represent actions for the model: continuous regression, discrete binning, and the DCT-based FAST scheme that compresses in the frequency domain.

What it is: the head emits floats directly; simple, but poor in multimodality if used with MSE.

Why learn: is the baseline against which everything else is measured.

Key concepts: regression, L1/L2, unimodality.

What it is: each dimension is divided into bins and treated as a class; gains multimodality, loses resolution and correlation between dimensions.

Why learn: was the dominant approach for years.

Key concepts: uniform binning, quantization, cross-entropy.

What it is: at 50Hz, tokens per timestep become nearly identical and redundant, creating extremely long sequences.

Why learn: is the pain point FAST solves.

Key concepts: temporal redundancy, long sequence, dynamic control.

What it is: applies DCT to the trajectory, quantizes coefficients, and compresses with BPE—short tokens in the frequency domain (PI, 2025).

Why learn: unlocks fast, faithful autoregressive training.

Key concepts: DCT, BPE, frequency-domain compression.

What it is: fewer tokens train faster but may lose fidelity; FAST moves the Pareto frontier.

Why learn: is the central engineering decision.

Key concepts: Pareto, sequence length, training speed.

What it is: a slow task can tolerate binning; a dynamic task calls for FAST or diffusion. The choice shapes the entire action head.

Why learn: wraps up Track 1 by connecting representation and architecture.

Key concepts: task dynamics, head design, representation decision.
