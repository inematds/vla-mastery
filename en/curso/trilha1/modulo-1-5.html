🌫️ Diffusion Policy and Flow Matching

Detailed content

Generate action as denoising: starts with Gaussian noise and refines it into an executable trajectory.

1

🎲 Why diffusion for action

In Module 1.4, we saw that MSE collapses modes. Diffusion solves this by modeling the action as a sampling from a conditional distribution: instead of predicting a value, it learns to transform Gaussian noise into a valid trajectory through denoising. As a sample, it naturally captures multimodal distributions — chooses one coherent mode instead of the average. And by generating the entire trajectory (chunk), it produces smooth, consistent movements.

💡 Three advantages of diffusion

Multimodal: represents “left OR right” without averaging. Trajectory: generates smooth chunks of future actions. Stable during training: the denoising objective is well-behaved, without GAN instability.

2

🌊 Diffusion Policy (2023)

Diffusion Policy (Chi et al., 2023) applied DDPM to control: a network (1D U-Net or transformer) denoises a sequence of future actions conditioned on the observation. Combines action chunking (predict H steps) with receding horizon (execute part, re-predict). It beat baselines in fine manipulation and became the gold standard for dexterous tasks—including Octo's action head.

# Diffusion Policy — inferência (esboço)
A = torch.randn(H, action_dim)          # ruído inicial
for k in reversed(range(K)):            # K passos de denoising
    eps = net(A, k, obs_cond)           # prevê o ruído
    A = ddpm_step(A, eps, k)            # remove um pouco
execute(A[:exec_horizon])               # receding horizon

📊 Why it worked

  • Multimodal — solves tasks with alternative solutions.
  • Stable — robust training across hyperparameters.
  • Chunking — smooth trajectories, less jitter.

📐 Score matching and the reverse process

Under the hood, diffusion has a forward process that adds Gaussian noise to the action over K steps until it becomes pure noise, and a reverse process learned to undo it. The network learns the score — the log-density gradient ∇log p(a) — equivalent to predicting the added noise. The conditioning the observation enters via FiLM or cross-attention, making generation depend on what the robot sees.

1

Forward: add noise

The clean action becomes Gaussian noise in K steps (defined by a schedule).

2

Learn the score

The network predicts the noise (≈ score), minimizing a simple regression error.

3

Reverse: sampling

Starting from noise, applies the score K times, conditioned on the observation.

⚡ Practical tip

Conditioning is what makes the policy reactive: without it, you sample plausible actions disconnected from the scene. FiLM is lightweight; cross-attention is more expressive. Choose based on your budget.

➡️ Flow Matching and its use in π0

Flow Matching generalizes diffusion: instead of a stochastic denoising process, it learns a vector field that transports noise→action through a Continuous ODE. With conditional flow matching, the training target is simply the velocity along a path (often straight) between noise and data—a simpler, more stable objective. The π0 uses exactly this in its action expert to generate smooth continuous actions at 50Hz.

✓ Advantages of flow matching

  • ✓Simpler and more stable training than DDPM.
  • ✓Straight paths require fewer inference steps.
  • ✓High-frequency continuous action (π0 at 50Hz).

✗ Precautions

  • ✗Still requires integrating the ODE (multiple NFEs).
  • ✗Sensitive to path parameterization.
  • ✗Requires a well-conditioned action expert.

⚡ Inference cost and distillation

The central cost of generative policies is number of function evaluations (NFE) — Each denoising/integration step is a forward pass. Classic DDPM uses tens to hundreds of steps, impractical at 50Hz. Solutions: fast samplers (DDIM), consistency models (generate in 1-2 steps) and distillation from a slow model to a fast one. Flow matching with straight paths already reduces NFE naturally.

📊 Step budget

  • 50-100 NFE — Naive DDPM; high quality, high latency.
  • ~10 NFE — DDIM / flow with a straight path; a good balance.
  • 1-2 NFE — distilled consistency models; real time.

💡 Latency vs. quality

Combine few NFEs with action chunking: generate a chunk with 5-10 steps and execute it while resampling. This lets you retain diffusion’s expressiveness within the real-time budget.

⚖️ Diffusion vs. autoregressive

The key design choice for the action head: diffusion/flow generation versus autoregressive tokens. Diffusion is more expressive (continuous multimodal output) but requires multiple steps. Autoregressive methods (RT-2, OpenVLA, FAST) reuse the LLM backbone and are simple to train, but generate one token at a time, and discretization can limit fidelity. There is no universal winner—the trade-off is expressiveness × latency × ease of integration.

Eixo            Difusão/Flow        Autoregressivo (tokens)
expressividade  alta (multimodal)   média (depende da disc.)
latência        K passos por chunk  1 forward, N tokens
integração      action expert extra reusa o backbone LLM
exemplos        Diffusion Policy,π0 RT-2, OpenVLA, FAST

⚡ Practical tip

Dexterous, continuous task → diffusion/flow. Simple integration on top of a VLM and unified autoregressive training → tokens (and Module 1.6 shows how FAST makes this competitive).
