🔹 Open X-Embodiment: 1M+ trajectories, 22 embodiments

O Open X-Embodiment (OXE, 2023) is the "ImageNet of robotics": according to the Open X-Embodiment paper (2023), a collaboration of dozens of labs that unified 60+ datasets in 1M+ trajectories through 22 robot bodies different — from table arms to mobile bases. This made it feasible to train generalist VLAs like OpenVLA and Octo.

📊 OXE in numbers (according to the paper, 2023)

  • 1M+ — real demonstration trajectories.
  • 22 — distinct embodiments (robot types).
  • 60+ — aggregated source datasets.
  • 2023 — corpus date; numbers as stated by the paper, not rechecked in Oct/2026. It remains the basis of OpenVLA/Octo, but in 2026 the largest pre-training runs already add human video (topic 6).
# OXE é distribuído via TFDS; cada fonte é um dataset RLDS
import tensorflow_datasets as tfds
b = tfds.builder_from_directory("gs://gresearch/robotics/fractal20220817_data/0.1.0")
ds = b.as_dataset(split="train")
# misturar várias fontes com pesos -> treino generalista
sources = ["fractal20220817_data", "bridge", "kuka", "taco_play"]  # subset OXE

⚡ Practical tip

The full OXE dataset is tens of TB. In practice, you select a mix from relevant sources (the "OXE magic soup" from OpenVLA) instead of downloading everything — what matters is embodiment and task coverage, not raw volume.

🔹 RT-X and cross-embodiment co-training

The central finding of the papers RT-X (RT-1-X, RT-2-X) was the positive transfer: training a single model on multiple bodies improves performance on each one compared with training in isolation. Data from a Franka arm helps generalization to a WidowX — knowledge "leaks" across embodiments.

✓ Do

  • ✓Co-train across multiple embodiments to generalize.
  • ✓Mix large datasets (OXE) with a few demos from your robot.
  • ✓Weight (sampling weights) datasets by quality/size.

✗ Avoid

  • ✗Assuming positive transfer without normalizing action spaces.
  • ✗Let a giant dataset dominate the sampling.
  • ✗Ignore embodiment bias (more data ≠ more diverse).

⚡ Practical tip

Positive transfer isn’t automatic: it depends on a shared action representation and mixing weights well chosen. It’s the empirical basis for why generalist VLAs work.

🔹 DROID: 76k demos, 564 scenes, in-the-wild

DROID (Distributed Robot Interaction Dataset, 2024) tackles the diversity: according to the DROID paper (numbers not rechecked in Oct/2026), ~76k trajectories collected in 564 scenes and 86 tasks across 13 institutions, with a standardized setup (Franka + RealSense). It is in-the-wild — real offices, kitchens, and labs — not a controlled table.

📊 Why diversity matters (numbers from the DROID paper, 2024)

  • 76k — trajectories on standardized hardware (Franka).
  • 564 — distinct scenes; robust visual generalization.
  • 86 — tasks; broad skill coverage.
  • in-the-wild — Real-world environments reduce overfitting to a single table.

⚡ Practical tip

Standardized hardware (Franka + Robotiq + RealSense) is the secret to DROID: the action space is consistent across all 564 scenes, so all the variance goes into what matters—scenario and task, not robot calibration.

🔹 LIBERO: continuous learning benchmark

LIBERO is less a training dataset and more a transfer benchmark: measures how a policy learns new tasks without forgetting old ones (lifelong learning). The four suites isolate dimensions — Spatial, Object, Goal e Long (long horizon). As of Oct/2026 LIBERO is saturated: OpenVLA-OFT 97.1% (02/2025), MolmoAct 2 97.2% and 98.1% in Think mode (05/2026), X-VLA 98.1% and, according to summaries, Galaxea G0.5 98.9% (2026). With all of them above 97%, it has become a sanity test, not a ranking. To compare state-of-the-art models, use the successors: LIBERO-Plus e RoboTwin 2.0 (harder versions), RoboCasa365 (compositional mobile manipulation), BEHAVIOR Challenge 2026 (100 complete household tasks, 20,000 demos, and 1,950 h of teleoperation), MolmoBot (Ai2) and the RoboArena (distributed real-world evaluation, pairwise A/B, global ranking).

📊 What each suite isolates

  • Spatial — same task, different spatial layout.
  • Object — same layout, different objects.
  • Goal — same scene, different instruction/goal.
  • Long — long horizons, composing subtasks.
# suites do LIBERO (cada uma isola uma variação)
libero_spatial   # mesma tarefa, layout espacial diferente
libero_object    # mesmo layout, objetos diferentes
libero_goal      # mesma cena, objetivo/instrução diferente
libero_long      # tarefas de horizonte longo (10 subtarefas)
# métrica: success rate por suite -> robustez de transferência

⚡ Practical tip

LIBERO is still the most-cited sanity test for VLA fine-tuning, but, being saturated, it is not enough on its own: also report a harder benchmark or real-world evaluation. A high number only in spatial and low in long reveals that the policy does not compose skills—a sign of weakness over long horizons.

🔹 Format and normalization: RLDS/TFDS, heterogeneous action spaces

Combining 60 datasets requires a common format: RLDS (Reinforcement Learning Datasets) on TFDS. The challenge is harmonization: each robot has its own action space (joints vs Δpose, binary vs continuous gripper, different frequencies). Without normalizing to a shared representation, co-training does not transfer. In the PyTorch ecosystem the de facto format is LeRobotDataset v3.0 (lerobot ≥ 0.4): Parquet + MP4 per camera, with depth and language columns since v0.6 (07/2026); there is a conversion script from the v2.1 format.

import tensorflow_datasets as tfds
# carregar uma fonte do OXE (formato RLDS)
ds = tfds.load("fractal20220817_data", split="train")  # RT-1 dataset

for ep in ds.take(1):
    for step in ep["steps"]:
        step["observation"]["image"]      # frame
        step["action"]                    # action space do robô-fonte
        step["language_instruction"]      # instrução textual
# harmonizar: normalizar ações p/ representação comum (ex: EEF Δpose 7D)

📊 Heterogeneity dimensions

  • Action space — joints vs. EEF Δpose vs. velocity.
  • Gripper — binary, continuous, force.
  • Frequency — from a few Hz to tens of Hz across datasets (e.g.: lerobot/pusht 10 fps, lerobot/aloha_mobile_cabinet 50 fps, read on 10/19/2026).
  • Cameras — number, resolution, viewpoint.

🔹 Curation and licenses: quality, bias, how to contribute

More data isn't better data. Curation — filtering failed demos and balancing coverage often works better than increasing raw volume. Pay attention to embodiment bias (datasets dominated by one robot) and sometimes licenses from each source. Contributing is simple: standardize on the format and publish to the Hub (81,292 datasets with the LeRobot tag as of 10/19/2026). The 2026 turning point is egocentric human video as the main source of pre-training: Figure's Index (08/2026) took Helix 2.5's zero-shot success from 9% to 56% in new homes (09/2026); NVIDIA cites "20,000+ hours" of human egocentric video in GR00T N1.7; EgoVerse (04/2026) is a platform and dataset of human egocentric data for robotics (per summaries, 1,362 h). Among open robot models, MolmoAct 2 (05/2026) published all of its data, including a bimanual YAM dataset with more than 720 h. License remains a filter: Index is closed.

1

Filter for quality

Remove failed/noisy demos; suboptimal ones confuse behavior cloning.

2

Check bias/license

Balance embodiments; respect the terms of each source dataset.

3

Contribute

Standardize (LeRobotDataset/RLDS) and publish to the Hub with a dataset card.

Your SO-101 demos (M2.2) can join this ecosystem. Standardized and published, they become fuel for the next VLA — and close the loop between tools and data.
