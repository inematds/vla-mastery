📐 Scaling laws for robotics

The billion-dollar question: VLA performance scales predictably with data and compute, as in LLMs? If there are clear scaling laws, the race becomes data collection at industrial scale. If not — if architecture or embodiment dominate — then ingenuity matters more than volume. As of Oct 2026 the evidence has gotten stronger, but it still comes from companies and not from independent replication: Figure says that the Helix 2.5 (Sep 2026) follows a human→humanoid transfer scaling law, with the loss predicted to 4 decimal places, and that pre-training on human video (Index) raises zero-shot success from 9% to 56%; Generalist AI reports, according to secondary sources, that GEN-0 (Nov 2025, ~270 thousand hours of real manipulation) scales with data and "ossifies" below 1B parameters.

💡 Why robotics differs from LLMs

Web text is practically free and abundant; robot trajectories are expensive and scarce. Even if scaling laws hold, the bottleneck is get the data. The 2026 turning point was the egocentric human video as the main source of pre-training (Figure's Index, Aug/2026; EgoVerse, Apr/2026; GR00T N1.7 with "20,000+ hours of human egocentric video"). World models and self-improvement (next topics) attack the same bottleneck.

🌍 World models and World Action Models: Genie 3, Cosmos 3, DreamZero

World models learn world dynamics—given a state and an action, predict the next state. Genie 3 (DeepMind, Aug/2025) generates interactive worlds in real time at 720p and 24 fps, consistent for a few minutes; V-JEPA 2 (Meta, Jun/2025, 1.2B, open) plans zero-shot pick-and-place in latent space. At NVIDIA, the separate 2025 lines (Predict/Reason/Transfer) were unified into Cosmos 3 (weights on HF on 05/31/2026, OpenMDW 1.1 license): Super 64B, Nano 16B, and Edge 4B (Edge runs on RTX, DGX, and Jetson Thor), which serves as a VLM, a simulator for synthetic data, or a policy backbone.

The new category of 2026 is the World Action Model (WAM): a single model jointly predicts future video and actions. The DreamZero (NVIDIA, arXiv Feb/2026) is a 14B autoregressive video diffusion model that, according to the paper, generalizes more than 2x better than state-of-the-art VLAs on new tasks and environments, runs in closed loop at 7 Hz, and adapts to a new body with 30 min of "play data". The GR00T N2 (preview at GTC, Mar 2026; release planned for late 2026, not yet released as of Oct 2026) uses this architecture, and the 1X World Model (14B video backbone) has been NEO's "cognitive core" since Jan/2026. Thesis: predicting future video teaches physics better than predicting actions alone. Genie 4 and V-JEPA 3 were not found as of Oct/2026.

📊 Two uses for world models

  • Generate data — synthesizing plausible physical trajectories at scale (Cosmos 3), reducing real-world collection.
  • Plan by imagining — the robot "dreams" futures in the model before acting (model-based RL; V-JEPA 2).
  • Being the policy itself — the WAM predicts video and action together (DreamZero, GR00T N2, 1X World Model). LeRobot 0.6 (Jul/2026) already ships policies of this kind: VLA-JEPA, FastWAM, and LingBot-VA.
  • Bridge to sim-to-real — complement Isaac/MuJoCo with learned rendering and dynamics.

♻️ Self-improvement: robots generating data

The holy grail is the self-improvement flywheel: robots that collect their own data via RL at scale and collection autonomy, improving without human demonstration with each cycle. Combined with world models, this can make continuous improvement cheap — the robotics equivalent of RLHF/RL in LLMs. Between Nov/2025 and Mar/2026 the RL in VLA post-training left the realm of theory: the Recap from π*0.6 (Physical Intelligence, Nov/2025) learns from corrections and advantage-conditioned policies and, according to PI, more than doubles throughput on hard tasks; the RL Token (RLT, Mar/2026) trains a small actor-critic online on top of a VLA token with a few hours, or minutes, of data; GR-RL (ByteDance, Dec/2025) turns a generalist into a specialist via RL. In LeRobot 0.6, the lerobot-rollout with a strategy dagger records human corrections during execution.

⚡ Why we’re not there yet

Real-world RL is expensive and risky (hardware breaks), and defining a reward for open-ended tasks is hard. World models reduce the cost by moving RL into the neural sim, but closing the flywheel reliably, with no human correcting, remains an open frontier — what to watch: online RL on more tasks beyond RLT's 4 precision tasks, and third-party numbers, not just the company's own.

🚢 Multi-robot and transfer: fleet learning

The natural consequence of a foundation model is one brain, many bodies. In the fleet learning, every robot in the field contributes data that improves the central model, and every OTA update improves the whole fleet. Combined with cross-embodiment (Track 2), an improvement on one arm can benefit a humanoid. In 2026 this showed up with numbers: the same checkpoint of the Gemini Robotics 2 (Jul/2026) runs on Apollo 2 with two different hands and on Franka Duo, and adapts to a new bimanual body "in a few hours", typically with fewer than 200 examples, according to DeepMind.

💡 The fleet's compounding advantage

It's the same network effect that made Tesla's FSD hard to catch up with: whoever has more robots in operation learns faster, which sells more robots. The fleet learning turns deployment scale into a model advantage—a moat that deepens over time.

🛡️ Safety and certification: ISO, fail-safe

No deployment at a scale close to human scale happens without certification. The picture as of Oct 2026, always per the source indicated: the ISO 10218-1:2025 (robots) and the ISO 10218-2:2025 (applications and cells) remain in two parts, replaced the 2011 versions and absorbed the content of the ISO/TS 15066 (cobots), keeping the 4 collaborative techniques — safety-rated monitored stop, hand guiding, speed and separation monitoring, and power and force limiting (according to secondary sources; iso.org could not be read). In the US, the ANSI/A3 R15.06-2025 adopts ISO 10218, with part 3 finalized in Jan/2026 (according to A3). For humanoids and robots with active balance, the ISO 25785-1 is still a draft (ISO/CD), with publication estimated for 2026–2027 — no manufacturer can claim certification against it (per Drives & Controls, Oct/2026). In the EU, the Digital Omnibus (political agreement in May/2026) removed machines from the AI Act's own high-risk list and moved the AI requirements into the Machinery Regulation (EU portal); the high-risk deadlines were postponed to Dec 2, 2027 (Annex III) and Aug 2, 2028 (Annex I), according to the EU portal and law firms. There is no ISO/IEEE standard specific to VLAs; what exists are 2026 academic benchmarks (RoboJailBench, SafeVLA-Bench, ForesightSafety-VLA, LIBERO-Safety) and company reports, such as the Gemini Robotics 2 safety technical report. The underlying challenge remains: how to certify a system learned and probabilistic? Requires deterministic fail-safes, monitoring guarantees, and physical alignment.

✓ Certification path

  • ✓Fail-safe deterministic around the learned policy (a rigid safety layer).
  • ✓Hardware-certifiable force/speed limits (power and force limiting, now within ISO 10218-2:2025).
  • ✓Runtime monitoring and guaranteed safe stopping.

✗ What it still doesn’t solve

  • ✗Guarantee a network's behavior in novel situations (out-of-distribution).
  • ✗Standards that still don't cover learned autonomy in open environments.
  • ✗Legal responsibility for autonomous decisions.

🔮 2030 Scenarios: what to watch

Where does all this lead us? Nobody has a source for predicting 2030; the scenarios below are working hypotheses, and what matters is knowing which signals to watch: GR00T N2 actually shipping; Optimus V3 appearing and production starting; 1X NEO delivering to customers; ISO 25785-1 being published; the AI Act deadlines (12/2027 and 08/2028) holding; real rankings such as RoboArena and BEHAVIOR 2026 rising with third-party evaluation. The scenarios range from transformer (general-purpose robots in factories, warehouses, and homes, productivity gains comparable to electricity) to incremental (slow deploy, stuck in the trough of disillusionment). The likely truth is somewhere in the middle—adoption sector by sector, starting with the mature cases in Module 3.4.

A

Accelerated scenario

Scaling laws hold + world models unlock data + fleet learning compounds. Signal: zero-shot like Helix 2.5's, replicated by third parties and on more tasks.

B

Base scenario

Sector adoption: logistics and manufacturing first, home later. Signal: pilots (BMW, GXO, Mercedes) turning into contracts with unit counts.

C

Skeptical scenario

The sim-to-real gap and certification hold things back; the sector spends years in the trough of disillusionment before maturing. Signal: overcapacity in China (NDRC warning, 2026) and valuation corrections.

You don't need to guess the right scenario — you need to be positioned for any of them. Mastering the stack (Tracks 1-2) and the strategy (Track 3) makes you valuable in both the boom and the downturn. That's the goal of VLA Mastery.
