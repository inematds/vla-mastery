📈 Size and projections: humanoids and AI robotics

Projections vary enormously because the market is still taking shape. Goldman Sachs estimated ~US$ 38B for humanoids alone by 2035; broader estimates for AI robotics reach US$ 111B+ by 2030. The exact number isn't what matters—the order of magnitude and the direction are: tens to hundreds of billions over a decade.

📊 Read projections with healthy skepticism

  • US$ 111B+ — broad estimate of AI robotics through 2030 (varies widely by methodology).
  • ~US$ 38B — Goldman for humanoids by 2035, in the base case.
  • Real TAM — if the target is global physical work, it’s trillions; the question is timing.

🔗 Value chain: chips, actuators, models, data

The value chain has layers: silicon (NVIDIA, edge), actuators and hands, foundation models, integrators e data. As on any platform, the margin tends to concentrate where there is defensible differentiation—and the consensus in 2026 is that operational data e models form the moat, while hardware becomes a commodity.

💡 Where the moat is

Humanoid hardware is getting cheaper (Unitree showed this). What isn't easily commoditized is data flywheel: those with robots in operation collect data that improves the model, which sells more robots. The data/model layer is the one that looks most like software in terms of margins.

🏷️ Business models: RaaS, licensing, data

How do you monetize? Three models dominate. RaaS (robot-as-a-service) charges by the hour/task, removing the client's CapEx and aligning revenue with the value delivered. Model licensing sells the brain to hardware OEMs. And data monetization turns operations into an asset that feeds back into the model.

⚠️ Risks: hype cycle, sim-to-real gap, regulation

Reading risks is as strategic as reading opportunity. The sector may enter the trough of disillusionment of the hype cycle if viral demos don't convert into profitable deployments. The residual sim-to-real gap, immature regulation, and the adoption timing are the main constraints. Anyone investing or building a career needs to model the pessimistic scenario.

⚡ The lesson of the hype cycle

Every transformative technology goes through a peak of inflated expectations, a trough of disillusionment, and only then a plateau of productivity. General-purpose robotics is near the peak in 2026. The winners will be those who survive the trough—usually those with real revenue, not just demos.

📊 The 4 risks to model

  • Hype cycle — delivery expectations pulled forward; valuation correction.
  • Residual sim-to-real gap — the last 10% of reliability is the most expensive.
  • Regulation — still immature; certification may delay deployment (see M3.6).
  • Timing — Enter too early and you burn cash; too late and you miss the window.
